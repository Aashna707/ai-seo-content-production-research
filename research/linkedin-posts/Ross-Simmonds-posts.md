# Ross Simmonds
**Content Reviewed:** 4 LinkedIn posts/articles

**Research Focus:** Content Distribution Strategy, Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), AI Citation & Visibility, Content Marketing Economics, Thought Leadership, Authority Building

## Why Selected

Ross Simmonds is the CEO of Foundation Marketing and Distribution.ai, and the author of
"Create Once, Distribute Forever." He is the leading voice on content distribution strategy
in B2B marketing, and has been among the earliest practitioners to articulate how GEO
(Generative Engine Optimization) changes the distribution game. His perspective is unique:
while most SEO experts focus on creation, Ross focuses on *distribution* — where content
lives, how it spreads, and how it gets cited. In the AI search era, distribution to
authoritative sources is not just a traffic tactic; it's an AI training data strategy.

## Post 1 — Why Unscalable Content Wins in the AI Era

**Date:** January 2024

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_why-creating-unscalable-content-amidst-the-activity-7155173252296790016-49hP

**Summary:**
Simmonds makes a counterintuitive argument: as AI makes content creation infinitely
scalable, the content that *can't* be easily scaled becomes the most valuable. Proprietary
research, original case studies, deep expert interviews, and documented first-hand
experiences are all forms of content that AI cannot replicate from existing sources. He
predicts that the winning SEO content of 2025 and beyond will be content that is hard to
scale — because that's the content AI systems will cite.

**Key Takeaways:**
- The paradox of AI content: AI makes it easy to produce volume, but volume without
  originality devalues the entire content category. The scarcity premium shifts to
  hard-to-produce original content.
- Content that is "hard to scale" — original research, documented experiments, expert
  interviews, proprietary data — is exactly what AI systems need to generate accurate,
  novel answers. This makes it citation-worthy.
- Video content is particularly hard to scale with AI (at least authentically) and
  represents a growing traffic opportunity as AI absorbs text-based query traffic.
- The long game: invest now in content formats that AI cannot easily replicate. The
  short-term ROI may be lower, but the compound effect is enormous.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This post reframes the AI content production challenge perfectly. The question is not
"how do we produce more content with AI?" but "what content can we produce that AI
cannot?" That's the content that gets cited in AI answers.

## Post 2 — Create Once, Distribute Forever: The GEO Application

**Date:** Ongoing (core framework, recent GEO application)

**URL:** https://ca.linkedin.com/in/rosssimmonds

**Summary:**
Simmonds applies his signature "Create Once, Distribute Forever" framework to GEO.
His argument: in traditional SEO, you create content and optimize it to rank. In GEO,
you create content and distribute it to the authoritative sources that AI systems train
on and cite. The distribution strategy is the AI SEO strategy. He also articulates the
2026 imperative: brands that are not cited in AI answers are invisible to an increasingly
large share of their addressable market.

**Key Takeaways:**
- GEO changes where distribution matters. The highest-value distribution targets are now
  sources that AI systems trust and cite: major publications, authoritative directories,
  high-domain-authority editorial outlets.
- The "Create Once" discipline is more important than ever: a single piece of original
  research distributed to 20 authoritative sources creates 20 citation opportunities for
  AI systems. Volume without distribution creates zero citations.
- "If you're not cited, you're invisible" — for a growing share of queries, the AI answer
  is the end of the user journey. Brands not cited in that answer don't exist.
- Organic Search is no longer just Google. AI engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity)
  are now discovery surfaces that require their own optimization strategy.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
The GEO distribution framework is the most actionable guidance available for content teams
asking "what do we do differently for AI search?" — and the answer is: distribute to
authoritative sources that AI cites, not just to channels that drive traffic. 

**Research Score:** 5/5.

## Post 3 — AI and Content Marketing: Should Agencies Charge Less?

**Date:** 2024

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_ross-simmonds-office-hours-what-will-content-activity-6884134918029369344-d1ST

**Summary:**
Simmonds addresses the common client question: "Can't you just use ChatGPT? Why does
content still cost the same?" His answer: AI reduces the *time* to produce content, but
the *value* of content has never been higher. AI increases output capacity, not content
quality — the quality premium remains human. He argues agencies and content teams using AI
should expect faster turnarounds and potentially higher quality, not lower prices, because
the strategic layer (distribution, positioning, original insight) is unchanged.

**Key Takeaways:**
- AI tools reduce production time but do not reduce the value of strategic content
  thinking. The cost of content is not the time to write it — it's the strategy behind
  it, the research informing it, and the distribution executing it.
- "Higher quality content and quicker turnarounds" is the value proposition of AI-assisted
  content production — not cheaper content. Teams that position it as a cost-reduction tool
  are leaving value on the table.
- The human contribution in AI-assisted content: strategy, original insight, quality
  control, voice preservation, and distribution planning. None of these are AI-automatable.
- The content market is bifurcating: commodity content (AI-produced, low differentiation)
  is getting cheaper; strategic content (expert-authored, original data, strong
  distribution) is getting more valuable.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This post frames the business case for investing in high-quality AI SEO content production
rather than just cheap AI content. The economics are clear: the strategic layer is where
the value (and the AI citations) lives. 

**Research Score:** 4/5.

## Post 4 — Content Mix Framework: Validated SEO + Thought Leadership + Backlink Content

**Date:** November 2023

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_a-turbulent-troubling-weekend-for-openai-activity-7133574940145971200-e2gl

**Summary:**
Simmonds shares his framework for an optimal company blog content mix: 30% Validated SEO
Content, 25% Thought Leadership, 25% Backlink-Driven Content, 10% Social Sharing Content,
10% Sales Enablement. This framework remains relevant in the AI era because each category
serves a different purpose in the AI SEO ecosystem: SEO content targets queries; thought
leadership builds E-E-A-T; backlink content earns authority signals; social content drives
distribution.

**Key Takeaways:**
- A diversified content mix serves multiple purposes simultaneously: driving search traffic,
  building authority, earning links (which are AI citation signals), and supporting sales.
- Thought leadership (25%) is the category most directly relevant to AI SEO — it's the
  original, expert-authored content that AI systems are most likely to cite.
- Backlink-driven content (25%) builds the authority signals that make all other content
  more citeable in AI answers.
- Validated SEO content (30%) — content designed around search volume and intent — is still
  the core of any content operation, even in an AI search world.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
The content mix framework is an operational planning tool. For AI SEO, the implication is
that the 25% thought leadership and 25% backlink content categories are the primary drivers
of AI citation probability. Increasing these allocations at the expense of pure SEO
content may be the right adaptation for AI-first search. 

**Research Score:** 4/5.

## Overall Insights

1. **Distribution is the AI SEO strategy.** Creating content is table stakes. Distributing
   it to sources that AI systems trust and cite is what earns AI visibility. "Create Once,
   Distribute Forever" is more relevant in the GEO era than in traditional SEO.

2. **Unscalable content is the scarcest and most valuable asset in an AI content world.**
   Original research, case studies, proprietary data, and expert interviews cannot be
   replicated by AI — they are what AI systems need to generate novel, accurate answers.

3. **AI tools increase capacity, not strategic value.** The human contribution (strategy,
   insight, quality control, distribution) remains unchanged in cost and importance. AI
   reduces time; strategy remains the differentiator.

4. **AI search is a parallel discovery surface.** ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity are not
   replacements for Google — they're additional surfaces that require separate optimization.
   Organic search is no longer just Google.

5. **The content mix for AI SEO skews toward thought leadership and backlink content.**
   These are the categories that build authority signals and original insights that AI
   systems cite. Pure SEO content volume is decreasingly sufficient on its own.
