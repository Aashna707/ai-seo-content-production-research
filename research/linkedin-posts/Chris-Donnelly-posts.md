## Post 2 — How LLMs Cite Content: The Complete Guide to AEO

**Date:** 2025

**URL:** https://uk.linkedin.com/in/donnellychris (active post)

**Summary:**
Donnelly publishes a framework for understanding how LLMs select and cite content. He
identifies five factors: (1) source authority (domain reputation in training data), (2)
content structure (clear, direct, schema-marked), (3) citation density (how many
authoritative sources reference the content), (4) recency (updated content preferred for
time-sensitive queries), and (5) intent alignment (how well the content's framing matches
how users phrase queries). He positions this as the practitioner's guide to AEO.

**Key Takeaways:**
- LLM citation selection is not random — it follows predictable patterns based on authority,
  structure, and intent alignment.
- Schema markup and structured content (FAQs, numbered lists, definition blocks) increase
  AI parsability and citation probability.
- "Intent alignment" is the gap most content teams miss: they write about topics the way
  their internal team describes them, not the way users actually search for them.
- Getting cited in AI answers drives revenue — Donnelly references teams generating
  significant pipeline from ChatGPT citations alone.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This is a practitioner's AEO checklist. The five citation factors are directly actionable
for content brief design: every brief should include authority signals, structure guidelines,
intent-aligned framing, and a freshness plan. 

**Research Score:** 5/5.

## Post 3 — 8 Prompt Frameworks for AI Content That Sounds Like You

**Date:** 2025

**URL:** https://uk.linkedin.com/in/donnellychris (active post — R-T-F framework)

**Summary:**
Donnelly shares 8 structured prompting frameworks for generating AI content that retains
authentic voice and professional quality. The frameworks include R-T-F (Role, Task,
Format), T-A-G (Task, Action, Goal), and several others. He claims to have used these
with 750+ founders inside his content academy. The insight: random prompting produces
generic content; structured prompting frameworks produce content that can realistically
replace or supplement human-written content.

**Key Takeaways:**
- R-T-F (Role, Task, Format): "Act as a [expert persona]. Write a [content type] about
  [topic]. Format it as [structure]." — The single most reliable prompting framework for
  professional content output.
- T-A-G (Task, Action, Goal): Best for quick drafts — define what you want done, how to
  do it, and what outcome you need. Minimal setup, fast results.
- Prompt engineering is a learnable, teachable skill that compounds. Teams that build a
  prompt library outperform teams that prompt from scratch every time.
- Voice preservation requires explicit instruction: prompt with examples of your own
  previous content and ask the model to match style.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
Prompt frameworks are the operational infrastructure of AI content production. For an SEO
content team producing at scale, standardized prompt templates are as important as editorial
guidelines — they ensure consistency and quality across writers and AI outputs. 

**Research Score:** 4/5.

## Overall Insights

1. **Structured prompting is the foundation of scalable AI content production.** Generic
   prompts produce generic content. Teams that invest in building and refining prompt
   frameworks outperform those that treat AI tools as simple text generators.

2. **LLM citation follows predictable patterns.** Authority, structure, citation density,
   recency, and intent alignment are the five factors. Every content brief should be
   designed with these factors explicitly addressed.

3. **AI tools are most valuable when integrated with operational data.** Content informed
   by real customer conversations, product feedback, and sales data is more relevant and
   more E-E-A-T aligned than content generated from generic briefs.

4. **The economics of content production have changed permanently.** AI reduces the
   marginal cost of content creation to near-zero. The competitive moat is no longer
   production speed — it's the quality of insight, data, and strategy behind the content.

5. **Prompt libraries are a strategic asset.** A team's library of tested, refined prompts
   for different content types is as valuable as an editorial style guide. It encodes
   institutional knowledge about what produces quality output.
