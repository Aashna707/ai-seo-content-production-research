## Post 1 — How to Create SEO Content That Ranks With ChatGPT (Step-by-Step)

**Date:** June 2023

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nathangotch_how-i-create-seo-content-that-ranks-with-activity-7077989962096259072-HHxF

**Summary:**
Gotch shares his complete AI-assisted SEO content workflow in a single LinkedIn post. The
process: (1) extract top 50 NLP keywords from competitor content using a structured ChatGPT
prompt, (2) generate an article using those keywords plus competitor heading structures,
(3) inject personal experience and expertise into the AI draft, (4) edit for Grammarly 95+
score. He provides the exact prompts he uses for each step, making this immediately
replicable.

**Key Takeaways:**
- Keyword extraction prompt: ask ChatGPT to analyze competitor content and extract top 50
  keyword phrases with relevance scores and frequency counts. Use the *average* across top
  3 competitors for a data-grounded brief.
- Heading research: pull competitor headings to understand the expected content structure
  for the query, then modify to differentiate.
- The critical step: "inject your own unique experience and expertise." AI draft + human
  insight = content that passes E-E-A-T filter. Pure AI draft = generic content.
- Edit discipline: target 95+ Grammarly score before publishing. Quality control is a
  measurable standard, not a vague aspiration.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This is one of the most replicable AI SEO content workflows shared by any practitioner.
The NLP keyword extraction, competitor analysis, and human enrichment steps form the
skeleton of an effective AI content production SOP.

## Post 2 — SEO in 2025: The Non-Negotiables

**Date:** January 2025

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nathangotch_seo-for-2025-1-create-original-content-activity-7278392661948964864-CzjN

**Summary:**
Gotch outlines his non-negotiable SEO principles for 2025. His first point: create original
content — not AI-rephrased or AI-summarized content. He argues that the sites winning in
2025 are publishing primary research, case studies, and tested advice that cannot be found
elsewhere. He also discusses the shift from traditional ranking (blue links) to GEO/AEO
(appearing in AI-generated answers) as a parallel track that SEOs must now manage.

**Key Takeaways:**
- "Classic SEO is not dead, but it's no longer the game we grew up with." AI Mode, AI
  Overviews, and ChatGPT search have added new surfaces that require different optimization.
- Original content — tested advice, proprietary data, documented case studies — is the
  differentiator in 2025. Commodity content doesn't rank or get cited in AI answers.
- GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) and SEO are not the same, but they're not
  mutually exclusive either. Structure, authority, and E-E-A-T serve both goals.
- Keyword-rich business names have an AI search advantage — early data shows AI assistants
  favor businesses whose names contain the service category.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
Gotch's articulation of "original content as the non-negotiable" is a useful north star for
AI content production. It frames the production challenge correctly: AI is a production
tool, not a content strategy. Strategy requires human judgment about what original insights
to generate.

## Post 4 — SEO vs GEO: What's Actually Different

**Date:** 2025

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/in/nathangotch/ (active recent post)

**Summary:**
Gotch draws a clear distinction between traditional SEO and GEO (Generative Engine
Optimization). SEO optimizes for ranked links in traditional SERPs; GEO optimizes for
inclusion in AI-generated answers. The overlap: authority, structure, and E-E-A-T serve
both goals. The differences: GEO requires more explicit entity signals, more structured
data markup, and content formatted for direct extraction (not just for click-through).
He also notes that earning a citation in AI answers does not guarantee brand mentions —
citation and mention tracking require separate tooling.

**Key Takeaways:**
- SEO and GEO are parallel disciplines. SEO targets the blue link; GEO targets the AI
  answer box. Both require authority and quality, but the content structure differs.
- For GEO: optimize for extractability. Write self-contained, definition-style paragraphs
  that can be pulled verbatim into AI answers without losing meaning.
- Entity signals (structured data, knowledge panel presence, consistent brand mentions
  across authoritative sites) are specifically important for GEO.
- Citation tracking is different from mention tracking. Being cited by AI (as a source
  link) and being mentioned by AI (as a brand reference) require different measurement
  approaches.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This SEO vs. GEO framework is exactly the kind of conceptual clarity that helps content
production teams make better decisions about how to structure and format their content.
**Research Score:** 5/5.

## Overall Insights

1. **The AI SEO content workflow has a proven structure.** Research competitors → extract
   NLP keywords → generate structured draft → inject human expertise → quality-gate with
   measurable standards. Gotch has made this replicable.

2. **GEO requires content optimized for extraction, not just ranking.** Self-contained
   paragraphs, schema markup, entity signals, and definition-style prose increase AI
   citation probability independently of traditional rank position.

3. **Tool selection should follow workflow design.** Gotch's AI-first stack works because
   the workflow was designed first. Teams that buy tools without redesigning workflows don't
   get the compound efficiency gains.

4. **Original content is the non-negotiable quality floor.** AI can draft structure, but
   the insight layer must be human-generated. Without it, content doesn't earn AI citations
   or sustained rankings.

5. **AI has changed the economics of SEO content production.** What previously required
   a full team (researcher, strategist, writer, editor) can now be accomplished by one AI-
   fluent person with the right workflow. The leverage is enormous — but so is the
   temptation to skip the human insight layer.
