# Kevin Indig
**Content Reviewed:** 4 LinkedIn Posts

**Research Focus:** AI Search, Enterprise SEO, AI Overviews, Content Defensibility

## Why Selected

Kevin Indig is a growth advisor (ex-Shopify, G2, Atlassian) who writes the Growth Memo
newsletter and advises enterprise and fast-growing startups on organic growth strategy. He
is one of the most rigorous thinkers in the space on how AI is changing search from an
*enterprise* perspective — not just tactically, but in terms of how content strategy,
metrics, and team structures need to change. His work on AI Mode, commodity vs. defensible
content, and the future of organic traffic is among the most cited in the industry.

## Post 1 — AI Mode: The Future of Traffic and SEO

**Date:** May 2025

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kevinindig_all-of-my-clients-have-asked-me-about-my-activity-7331421919147114496-qH3V

**Summary:**
Indig shares his perspective on Google's AI Mode — the new full AI-powered search
experience. He argues that AI Mode fundamentally breaks the link between search and
traffic. His clients (enterprise companies) are all asking the same question: how do we
prepare? His answer: stop optimizing for clicks and start optimizing for *demand creation*.
SEO in the AI Mode era is about influencing what users think and want before they search,
not just capturing queries.

**Key Takeaways:**
- AI Mode changes the metric: traffic is no longer the goal, demand creation and brand
  visibility are.
- Organic results are becoming "one of many answers" in a feed-like SERP — they no longer
  dominate the page.
- Content that is *unique, data-rich, and hard to copy* will earn AI citations. Commodity
  content gets absorbed by AI and never attributed.
- Enterprise teams need to adapt their reporting: clicks and impressions are no longer
  sufficient KPIs for organic search performance.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This post captures the strategic inflection point. For content teams building AI SEO
workflows, the implication is clear: invest in original research, proprietary data, and
expert-authored content that AI cannot synthesize from generic sources. Score: 5/5.

## Post 2 — Commodity Content vs. Defensible Content in the AI Era

**Date:** December 2023

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kevinindig_ai-content-seo-activity-7137469480791621633-XCxZ

**Summary:**
Indig argues that AI has driven the marginal value of commodity content to zero. Any blog
post that aggregates existing information can now be generated by ChatGPT in seconds.
The only content that retains strategic value is content that is technically enriched
(proprietary data, unique analysis) or human-differentiated (authentic perspective,
original experience). He frames this as a fork: either become technically elite at data
enrichment, or focus entirely on differentiation.

**Key Takeaways:**
- "If your content is easy to copy, you're dealing with commodities. If it's hard to copy,
  your marketing strategy is defensible."
- The bar for ranking and being cited in AI answers is rising: AI-generated commodity
  content competes with itself and loses.
- Smart content teams are doing one of two things: (1) enriching AI content with unique
  data to make it non-replicable, or (2) abandoning the commodity game entirely and going
  deep on thought leadership.
- Human + AI hybrid is the near-term winning model — AI for drafting and structuring,
  humans for insight, data, and voice.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This is the clearest framework for how to design an AI content workflow that actually
works. The implication: every AI-generated piece needs a "defensibility layer" — something
only your organization can contribute. Without that layer, the content gets lost in the AI
answer and never attributed. Score: 5/5.

## Post 3 — Reverse-Engineering Google's SGE (AI Overviews)

**Date:** June 2023

**URL:** https://mk.linkedin.com/posts/kevinindig_google-seo-ai-activity-7029090843068170240-8TPB

**Summary:**
Indig documents 11 observations from extensively testing Google SGE (now AI Overviews)
after getting early access. He breaks down the mechanics: how Google pulls passages from
web content to construct AI answers (similar to Featured Snippet logic), how product
carousels work inside AI results, and how the "Corroboration" feature can be used to
reverse-engineer which sources Google trusts for AI answers.

**Key Takeaways:**
- AI Overviews work similarly to Featured Snippets — Google takes passages of text from
  pages to construct answers. Optimizing for one helps the other.
- The Corroboration feature shows which websites Google cites in an AI answer — a direct
  tool for competitive AI SEO research.
- E-commerce queries get product carousels inside AI results, suggesting AI will eventually
  absorb commercial intent queries too.
- Unstructured review data from 3rd-party sites gets structured by Google's AI — meaning
  your presence on review platforms influences AI answers even if you didn't write the
  content yourself.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This is an early but still highly relevant framework for how AI Overviews select and cite
content. The passage-level optimization insight is actionable: structure content with clear,
citable paragraphs that answer specific questions. Score: 5/5.

## Post 4 — Programmatic SEO Is Dead; Human-AI Hybrid Is the Near-Term Winner

**Date:** July 2023

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kevinindig_seo-programmaticseo-ai-activity-7082354507581444097-2uT8

**Summary:**
Indig engages with criticism that programmatic SEO (pSEO) is essentially a spam technique
that AI has now made even easier to abuse. He agrees that the technical barrier to pSEO
has collapsed — AI can now generate templated content at scale. But he argues that the
*content quality* element of pSEO remains the biggest challenge even with AI. The best
near-term approach is human-AI collaboration: AI handles structure and drafting, humans
validate and inject expertise.

**Key Takeaways:**
- The technical barriers to programmatic SEO are gone — anyone can now generate thousands
  of pages with AI. This means differentiation must come from content quality, not scale.
- Google is actively devaluing scaled, low-quality AI content through Helpful Content
  Updates. The volume game is over.
- The winning workflow: AI generates → human validates and enriches → human reviews quality.
- Enterprise sites benefit most from AI-assisted SEO because one workflow change can improve
  thousands of pages simultaneously.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This post documents the transition moment from "AI replaces SEO writers" to "AI assists SEO
writers." For building a content production workflow, this frames the editorial QA layer
as non-negotiable. Score: 4/5.

## Overall Insights

1. **AI has bifurcated the content market.** Commodity content has near-zero strategic
   value. Only technically enriched or human-differentiated content earns AI citations
   and sustained rankings.

2. **The traffic metric is breaking.** Indig's enterprise clients are all facing the same
   problem: how to measure organic success when AI absorbs the query without producing a
   click. New metrics (AI citation frequency, demand creation signals, branded search) need
   to replace raw traffic reporting.

3. **Reverse-engineering AI Overviews is possible.** Google's AI answer sources follow
   passage-level logic similar to Featured Snippets. Content structured as citable,
   self-contained paragraphs is more likely to be pulled into AI answers.

4. **The human-AI hybrid model is the current consensus best practice.** AI drafts and
   structures; humans validate, enrich with data, and inject authentic perspective. Removing
   the human layer produces content that competes in a race to the bottom.

5. **Enterprise SEO now requires a demand-creation mindset.** The goal is not clicks — it
   is shaping what potential customers believe and search for. AI SEO is really brand
   strategy executed through content.
