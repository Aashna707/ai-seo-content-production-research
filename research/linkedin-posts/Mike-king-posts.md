## Post 1 — How AI Mode Works: Query Fan-Outs and Relevance Engineering

**Date:** May 2025

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelkingphilly/ (active post thread)

**Summary:**
King shares his breakdown of Google's AI Mode — the full AI-powered search experience that
launched in early 2025. He explains the "query fan-out" mechanism: when a user types a
query into AI Mode, Google's system expands it into dozens of sub-queries, runs them in
parallel, and synthesizes the results. This means content must not just answer one question
but must cover the full semantic neighborhood of a topic to be eligible for inclusion. He
also introduces his Qforia tool, which uses Gemini to simulate query fan-outs and reveal
what multi-query content an SEO needs to create.

**Key Takeaways:**
- AI Mode uses "query fan-out": one user query becomes 20-50 sub-queries. Content that
  doesn't cover the full topic cluster gets excluded from AI answer construction.
- This validates topical authority as an AI SEO requirement, not just a traditional SEO
  one. You must cover topics comprehensively to be eligible for AI citations.
- Qforia (King's tool) generates the likely query fan-outs for any topic — giving SEOs
  a content gap map for AI Mode optimization.
- Personal context (Google account history, location, past searches) is now factored into
  AI Mode results — personalized search is not a future concept, it's happening now.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
Query fan-out is the technical mechanism that makes topical authority matter in AI search.
Understanding this changes how content briefs are written: instead of targeting one keyword,
you target the full semantic cluster the AI will fan out to when processing that keyword.

## Post 2 — Relevance Engineering: SEO Is Dead, Engineering Begins

**Date:** April 2025 / ongoing

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelkingphilly/ (Relevance Engineering framework posts)

**Summary:**
King introduces and evangelizes his "Relevance Engineering" framework — a reframing of
SEO as a technical discipline combining information retrieval science, AI/NLP, UX, digital
PR, and content strategy. His argument: traditional SEO is like being a car mechanic
(fixing existing systems). Relevance Engineering is like being a car engineer (building
systems from first principles). He calls for SEOs to adopt vector embeddings, cosine
similarity analysis, topic segmentation, and semantic scoring as standard practice.

**Key Takeaways:**
- Relevance Engineering integrates: information retrieval (how search engines rank),
  NLP/AI (how machines understand language), UX (how users behave), digital PR
  (authority signals), and content strategy (what to create and why).
- SEOs must move beyond checklists and adopt experimental, data-driven frameworks.
  The industry has been "the janitors of the web" — cleaning up sites — when it should
  be engineering relevance from the ground up.
- Cosine similarity (measuring how semantically close content is to a query) is a
  measurable, optimizable signal for AI search ranking.
- The content team of the future combines SEO, NLP, data science, UX, and digital PR
  under one "Relevance Engineering" function.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This framework is the most comprehensive articulation of what AI SEO content production
needs to become. If your content production process doesn't include semantic analysis and
information retrieval thinking, it's optimizing for a search engine that no longer exists.
**Research Score:** 5/5.

## Post 3 — Inside Google's AI Patents: What They Reveal About AI Search

**Date:** June 2025

**URL:** https://searchengineland.com/michael-king (from his Search Engine Land article shared on LinkedIn)

**Summary:**
King publishes a deep-dive analysis of six Google patents that reveal how AI Overviews
and AI Mode actually work at the algorithmic level. Key findings: Google uses a multi-stage
RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) pipeline; content is evaluated at the *passage level*
(not just the page level); and the system weighs "information gain" — how much novel,
non-redundant information a passage adds to the answer.

**Key Takeaways:**
- Google's AI uses RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation): it retrieves relevant passages,
  then generates an answer. Content must be *retrievable at the passage level* to be
  included.
- "Information gain" is a real ranking signal in AI search: content that adds new
  information — not just repackaging what already exists — scores higher for AI citation.
- Passages (fraggles) are evaluated independently of the page they live on. A highly
  relevant paragraph on a mediocre page can still be cited by AI.
- The LLM model reasoning that Google uses mirrors how you'd prompt an AI yourself: it
  looks for structured, direct, confident answers to specific questions.

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
Patent analysis is the gold standard for understanding actual search mechanics. The passage-
level evaluation and information gain signals are directly actionable for content production:
write self-contained paragraphs, add novel data, and avoid repeating what every other source
already says. 

**Research Score:** 5/5.

## Post 4 — How LLMs Select Agencies: The Long-List-Then-Filter Pattern

**Date:** May 2026

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/in/michaelkingphilly/ (recent active post)

**Summary:**
King installs Gemma 4 12B locally and tests how a Google-built model reasons about
selecting "top SEO agencies." With "Show thinking" expanded, he documents the model's
reasoning process: it builds a long candidate list from training data, annotates each entry
with reputation signals, then filters down to a final ranked list. The insight: LLMs use
a "long-list-then-filter" pattern that mirrors human expert judgment. Brands that appear
frequently in authoritative training data are more likely to make the long list.

**Key Takeaways:**
- LLMs construct answers through explicit reasoning: candidate generation → annotation →
  filtering. Understanding this process tells you how to influence each stage.
- The "long list" phase is driven by training data frequency. More mentions in high-
  authority sources = higher probability of making the candidate list.
- The filtering phase is driven by reputation signals: awards, case studies, notable clients,
  third-party recognition.
- For brand AI visibility: you need both *frequency of mention* (volume in authoritative
  sources) and *quality of signal* (what those mentions say about you).

**Relevance to AI SEO:**
This is a concrete, observed mechanism for how LLMs select and recommend content and
brands. For AI SEO content production, the implication is clear: create content that
earns third-party citation (digital PR), not just content that ranks. 

**Research Score:** 5/5.

## Overall Insights

1. **AI search operates at the passage level, not the page level.** Google's RAG pipeline
   retrieves and evaluates individual paragraphs. Content teams must write self-contained,
   citable paragraphs — not just well-structured pages.

2. **Information gain is the AI-era differentiator.** Passages that add novel, non-
   redundant information score higher for AI inclusion. Every piece of content needs
   something that doesn't exist anywhere else — original data, tested conclusions,
   proprietary analysis.

3. **Query fan-out makes topical authority mandatory for AI search.** AI Mode expands
   queries into sub-queries. Only content that covers the full semantic neighborhood of a
   topic survives the fan-out process. Narrow keyword targeting is insufficient.

4. **Relevance Engineering is the next generation of SEO.** Combining information
   retrieval science, NLP, and content strategy into a unified technical discipline is where
   the field is heading. Teams that adopt this framework early will have a structural
   competitive advantage.

5. **LLMs use a long-list-then-filter pattern.** Brand visibility in AI answers is driven
   by frequency of mention in authoritative sources + quality of those mentions. Digital PR
   is now an AI SEO tactic, not just a link-building tactic.
