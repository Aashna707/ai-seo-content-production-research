# Kevin Indig — YouTube Research

## Video 1

**Title:** How Users Really Interact With AI Search — And What to Do About It

**Channel/Source:** The Campaign Podcast by 97th Floor

**YouTube URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gFqNOCWbF-4

**Transcript/Recap URL:** https://the-campaign.castos.com/episodes/how-users-really-interact-with-ai-search-and-what-to-do-about-it-w-kevin-indig-organic-growth-advisor

**Date:** May 30, 2025

**Duration:** 31 minutes

**Selection Score:** 5/5

## Why This Video

Indig ran the first large-scale UX study of Google AI Overviews — 70 real users, 8 search
tasks, 29 hours of recorded think-aloud sessions, 400+ AI Overview encounters. This is
primary observational data, not opinion. It directly overturns assumptions that most SEO
teams operate on (e.g. that "answer-first content" performs better, or that AI Overview
citations drive clicks). For anyone building an AI SEO content workflow, this is the most
empirically grounded reference available on how users actually behave with AI search.

---

## Key Transcript Excerpts

**On why the study was needed:**
> "Nobody has really ever observed users while interacting with AI Overviews. And I will go
> so far as to say that my mental model of SEO changed significantly as a result of this
> study."

**On trust as the primary filter:**
> "Users evaluate search results whether in the AI Overviews or outside with a two-step
> filter. The first filter is trust — they only look at results from brands they recognize.
> Without trust, relevance never even gets evaluated."

**On clicks as "empty calories":**
> "Clicks are empty calories — they're always just a means to an end... Only 20% of the
> time does the AIO give participants the final answer. But 80% of the time they clicked on
> other results, mostly organic results."

**On "answer-first content" backfiring:**
> "We tested content structured to deliver the answer immediately. What we found is that
> users who received 'answer-first' content actually bounced faster and expressed less trust.
> They wanted to see the reasoning, the evidence, the context."

**On brand mentions vs. citations:**
> "Being mentioned in an AI Overview — even without a citation link — is more valuable than
> being a citation link that no one clicks. Users see the brand name and register it."

## Video 2

**Channel/Source:** Churn.fm Podcast, Episode 295

**Transcript URL:** https://www.churn.fm/episode/the-future-of-seo-in-an-ai-first-world-with-kevin-indig

**Date:** June 2025

**Duration:** ~45 minutes

**Selection Score:** 5/5

## Why This Video

Indig's clearest articulation of the "2024 was peak traffic year" thesis and what comes
after. He explains the strategic pivot from traffic acquisition to influence — and spells
out exactly what content survives in an AI-first search world: original data, first-hand
experience, authority, and expertise. Everything else gets absorbed by AI with no
attribution.

---

## Key Transcript Excerpts

**On 2024 as peak traffic year:**
> "I'm always saying 2024 was peak traffic year. The clicks that still arrive on your
> website will be highly qualified because people will have all their questions answered
> through ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Google AI Overviews. They come to your site to validate
> that these answers are correct, to check out your offering — really close to a purchase
> decision. The benefits are that the traffic you still get is very high signal and low
> noise."

**On the disappearing middle layer:**
> "We're losing a middle layer of tracking and influence — the traffic that's very undecided.
> They would usually spend time on our website checking out different landing pages, maybe
> reading blog articles. That kind of goes away."

**On the strategic shift to influence:**
> "We're moving more towards a model of influence. Influence in the search results, influence
> on ChatGPT, influence on other platforms like Reddit and YouTube. It has a whole set of
> secondary effects in terms of which channels we invest in and how we create and structure
> our content."

**On what content survives:**
> "This classic evergreen model of content — where you write guides or explainers or
> tutorials — that completely goes away. And what remains is original data, first-hand
> experience, authority, and expertise."

**On trust as a baseline filter in high-stakes search:**
> "Humans scan the search results in AI answers for brands that they know and trust. And
> that matters more when the stakes behind the search are high — side effects of a
> medication, loans, mortgages. You arrive with a higher baseline of skepticism."

## Structured Key Takeaways (Across Both Videos)

1. **Trust is the primary filter for AI search results.** Unrecognized brands are
   invisible in AI Overviews regardless of citation position. Brand building is now an
   AI SEO prerequisite, not a separate marketing function.

2. **2024 was peak organic traffic.** Traffic volume is declining permanently. Teams
   planning content ROI around traffic projections need to rebuild their models.

3. **Clicks are empty calories — brand mentions are the new impression.** AI Overview
   mentions without clicks still build brand recall that converts in later sessions. The
   value model shifts from immediate click to cumulative visibility.

4. **"Answer-first content" actively reduces trust.** Users want reasoning, evidence, and
   context — not just the answer. Structuring content to show the work matters.

5. **The middle layer of undecided traffic is disappearing.** AI handles top-of-funnel
   informational queries. Traffic reaching your site is close-to-purchase. Content and UX
   must be calibrated for high-intent visitors, not awareness-stage readers.

6. **Evergreen guides, explainers, and tutorials lose their traffic function.** AI
   synthesizes these categories. What survives: original data, primary research, first-hand
   documented experience, and expert perspective that AI cannot produce from existing
   web content.

7. **Influence replaces traffic as the strategic objective.** Measure brand mention
   frequency in AI answers and share of voice in category queries — not organic traffic
   volume.

---

## Relevance to AI SEO Content Production Playbook

- **Content brief design:** Every brief needs a trust-building section — credentials,
  evidence, real documented cases — not just an answer structure.
  
- **Content format:** Show reasoning and evidence before conclusions. Users disengage
  from purely answer-first formats.
  
- **KPI framework:** Replace traffic metrics with AI mention frequency, share of voice
  in AI-generated category answers, and branded search volume.
  
- **Content prioritization:** Shift budget from evergreen guides to original research,
  case studies, and data-rich content that AI must cite.
  
- **Brand strategy:** AI SEO requires brand recognition. Content distributed to
  authoritative sources builds the recognition that makes AI citations valuable.
